# Work

Work is anything that moves an intent forward.

It is the fundamental unit of execution in Navo.

---

## What Is Work?

Work is not just a task. Work is any action — physical, mental, or logistical — that brings an intent closer to completion.

**Examples of work:**

- Write the shopping list
- Find a shop that sells fresh produce nearby
- Travel to the market
- Buy the tomatoes, spinach, and onions
- Return home
- Chop the vegetables

Each of these is a distinct piece of work. Some require physical presence at a location. Some require a decision first. Some can only happen after others are done.

---

## Work Is Discovered, Not Defined Upfront

When you form an intent — "get vegetables for dinner" — you do not yet know all the work involved.

You discover it progressively.

The first work item might be: "decide what to cook."

Only once that is done does the next work item become clear: "write the shopping list."

Only once the list is written does "find the nearest shop with these items" make sense.

Planning is the process of discovering this work step by step, not defining it all at once.

---

## Work Happens at Locations

Most work in daily life happens somewhere physical.

Location is a first-class property of work — not a tag, not a note, but a structural attribute that determines when and how work can be done.

**Examples:**

- "Buy vegetables" → at a market or supermarket
- "Pick up dry cleaning" → at the dry cleaner
- "Submit documents" → at the government office (open 10am–5pm)
- "Have a meeting" → at the office or a café

When work has a location, Navo can connect it to your route and tell you whether it is accessible today.

Work without a location is abstract. Work with a location is actionable.

---

## Work Has Dependencies

Some work can only begin after other work is completed.

**Example chain:**

1. Decide what to cook →
2. Write the shopping list →
3. Find a nearby shop →
4. Travel to the shop →
5. Buy the items →
6. Return home →
7. Prepare the meal

Understanding these dependencies is more important than managing an isolated list.

Navo models work as a graph of relationships, not a flat list.

---

## Types of Work

| Type            | Description                     | Example                               |
| --------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Task**        | A clear, actionable step        | "Write the shopping list"             |
| **Decision**    | Choosing between alternatives   | "Choose which market to visit"        |
| **Errand**      | Going somewhere to do something | "Visit the market"                    |
| **Research**    | Gathering information           | "Check what vegetables are in season" |
| **Purchase**    | Acquiring something             | "Buy tomatoes and spinach"            |
| **Preparation** | Making something ready          | "Chop the vegetables"                 |

---

## Work and the Route

The route you plan for the day determines which work is possible.

If the market is on your route → buying vegetables is easy.

If the market requires a 20-minute detour → you need to weigh whether that is worth it today.

If you have a free 30 minutes near a relevant location → a piece of work that was waiting becomes possible.

This is the core of day planning in Navo: work is enabled or disabled by your route.

---

## Work Is Connected Across Intents

Multiple intents can share work.

If you are already going to the supermarket for dinner vegetables, the trip can also fulfil "buy coffee for tomorrow morning" — a separate intent.

Navo surfaces these overlaps and helps you combine work efficiently into a single day.
