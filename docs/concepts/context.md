# Context

Context is everything that is true at the moment a decision is made.

It is not added to the plan later. It arrives with the intent from the very first moment.

---

## Context Comes First

When someone decides they want to "get vegetables for dinner," they already know:

- How many people they are cooking for
- Roughly what they want to make
- How much they want to spend
- What time dinner needs to be ready

This is context. It exists before any work is discovered and before any plan is formed.

Navo treats context as a first-class property — present at intent formation, actively used throughout planning.

---

## The Two Types of Context

### Intent Context

Context that comes with the intent itself:

- **Who**: number of people, dietary needs, preferences
- **Budget**: financial constraints
- **Timing**: deadlines, desired completion time
- **Constraints**: things that cannot change

This context stays relatively stable. It shapes what work is relevant.

### Day Context

Context that changes throughout the day:

- **Location**: where you are right now
- **Route**: where you are heading
- **Time**: how much time is available at each point
- **Energy**: your capacity at a given moment
- **Openings**: what is nearby and accessible on your route

This context is dynamic. It changes constantly and directly determines what opportunities are available.

---

## Context Changes What Is Possible

The same intent produces different optimal actions depending on context.

**Intent:** "Buy vegetables for dinner (4 people, ₹400 budget)"

**Context A:** You are driving past a farmers' market at 5pm, 30 minutes free.
→ Stop now. Fresh produce, on budget, on route. Done.

**Context B:** You are at the office, 2 hours until dinner, market is 4km away.
→ Order delivery. Or plan a stop on the way home.

**Context C:** You are running late, 15 minutes to dinner time.
→ Use what is already at home, or choose a faster alternative.

Same intent. Completely different actions. Context is why.

---

## Location Is the Most Important Contextual Variable

In day planning, your physical location — and where you are heading — is the single most powerful determinant of what work is possible.

This is why the **route** matters so much.

The route through locations defines:

- Which shops or places you can access without a detour
- Which windows of time are available at each stop
- Which opportunities naturally arise as you move

Without location context, a plan is abstract.

With location context, a plan becomes actionable.

---

## Context and Opportunities

When context aligns with available work — the right location, the right time, the right capacity — an **opportunity** is created.

Navo uses context continuously to surface these opportunities:

- You are passing a pharmacy → "collect prescription" becomes viable
- You have 20 minutes near a print shop → "print documents" can be done now
- Your route takes you by the market → "buy vegetables" is on the way

Opportunities disappear when context changes. Missing a window means rethinking the plan.

---

## Key Principle

> Context does not change the work itself. It changes what makes sense to do, when, and where.

Navo continuously reads context — especially location and route — to surface the right work at the right moment.
