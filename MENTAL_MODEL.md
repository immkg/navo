# Mental Model

Navo is built around the belief that software should model the way people naturally plan their days, not the way databases store information.

People do not think in tasks, projects, tickets, or folders.

They think in outcomes, movement, places, and moments.

Navo models these concepts directly.

---

## The Journey Begins With Intent

Everything starts with an intent.

An intent is something you want to accomplish. It is the outcome you have in mind before you know exactly how you will achieve it.

**Example:**

> "Get vegetables for dinner."

At the moment you form this intent, you do not yet know:

- Which vegetables to buy
- Which shop to visit
- How much it will cost
- When you will do it

But you do already know something: **context**.

You know how many people are eating. You know roughly what you want to cook. You know your budget. You know where you are going to be today.

This is the nature of intent. It exists before the plan. It carries context from the very beginning.

---

## Context Comes With the Intent

Context is not added to work later. It arrives with the intent itself.

When you decide to "get vegetables for dinner," you already carry:

- **Who**: how many people are eating tonight
- **Budget**: what you are willing to spend
- **Timing**: what time dinner needs to be ready
- **Constraints**: dietary needs, preferences, what is already at home

Context shapes every decision that follows.

The same intent — "get vegetables for dinner" — will result in completely different work depending on context.

---

## Work Is Discovered, Not Defined Upfront

To fulfil an intent, work must happen.

But work is not defined all at once. It is discovered progressively.

**Example sequence for "get vegetables for dinner":**

1. Figure out what to cook
2. Check what is already at home
3. Write a shopping list
4. Find a shop that is open and nearby
5. Travel to the shop
6. Buy the vegetables
7. Return home
8. Prepare the meal

You did not need to know steps 5 through 8 when you first formed the intent.

Each step became clear only as you made progress.

Planning is continuous discovery. Not upfront specification.

---

## The Day Is the Unit of Execution

The real heart of Navo is **day planning**.

People do not execute plans in the abstract. They execute them in a day, in real locations, moving through time and space.

A day is defined by three things:

| Dimension    | What it captures                       |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| **Time**     | When things can happen                 |
| **Location** | Where you are and where you need to be |
| **Route**    | How you move between locations         |

The route is not just travel. It is the spine of the day.

Every movement is an opportunity. Every location change can enable or disable work.

---

## The Route Enables and Disables Work

This is the core insight of Navo.

As you move through your day along a route, your proximity to locations determines what work becomes possible.

**Example:**

You are planning to travel from home → office → gym.

- Passing a farmers' market on the way to the office → **"buy vegetables"** becomes possible
- Arriving at the gym without stopping → **"buy vegetables"** is no longer possible until you return home
- A detour adds 20 minutes → some work becomes feasible, other work may need to move to tomorrow

The route is not just getting from A to B.

The route is a sequence of **opportunities** — moments where work can be performed if the conditions are right.

Navo uses the map and the planned route to surface these opportunities at the right moment.

---

## The Map Is Central

The map is not a feature in Navo.

It is the fundamental interface through which day planning becomes real.

Planning without a map is incomplete. You cannot know:

- Whether the shop is on your route or out of the way
- Whether two errands can be combined into one trip
- Whether the timing of your route allows work to be done

The map translates abstract work into physical reality.

It answers the question: **can this actually be done today, and when?**

---

## Opportunities Are Discovered Through Movement

When you plan your route for the day, Navo surfaces opportunities.

An opportunity is work that becomes easier, cheaper, or simply possible because of where you are or where you are going.

Examples:

- You are passing a bookshop → "pick up the book I ordered" becomes possible
- You will be near the pharmacy → "collect the prescription" can be added to today
- You are driving past a printer → "print the documents" is a natural stop

Opportunities are not pushed at you randomly.

They appear when the context — your location, route, time, and remaining work — makes them genuinely relevant.

---

## The Navo Loop

```
Intent forms (with context)
        ↓
Work is discovered progressively
        ↓
Day is planned: locations + time + route
        ↓
Route surfaces opportunities
        ↓
Work is executed as you move
        ↓
Completion reshapes the remaining plan
        ↓
New intents emerge
```

This loop runs continuously throughout the day.

It is not a project management system.

It is a living model of how your day unfolds.

---

## One Model, Many Views

The underlying model never changes. Different views simply answer different questions.

| View            | Answers                                    |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Intent**      | What am I trying to achieve?               |
| **Work Graph**  | What needs to happen and in what order?    |
| **Map**         | Where can it happen and is it on my route? |
| **Day Planner** | What should I do today and when?           |
| **Timeline**    | How does it unfold over time?              |

Every view is a window into the same connected model.

Changing the view never changes the underlying reality.
